### Why it is a category shift (or failure)

Classical “physics” assumes:

- Systems have well-defined properties

- Objects persist through time

- Causes precede effects in a locally intelligible way

- Measurement reveals what’s already there

Quantum theory quietly abandons or warps all four.

Instead of describing things, it often describes:

- State vectors rather than objects

- Probabilities rather than properties

- Measurement interactions rather than observations

- Constraints on knowledge rather than underlying realities

Calling that “physics” invites people to assume it’s the same kind of enterprise as Newton or Maxwell, just more precise or more microscopic. But structurally, it’s doing something very different.

So Yes—category shift feels right. We’re still using the old noun (“physics”) while the verb has changed.

### Where the shift actually happened

Historically, the shift wasn’t just:

“We found weird particles.”

It was:

“The theory no longer tells us what is, only what can be said about outcomes.”

That’s not just new content; that’s a new epistemic role for the theory itself.

### A sharper way to put it

You could almost argue that:

- Classical physics = ontology-forward

- Quantum theory = prediction-constraint machinery

Same lab coats, radically different conceptual job.

So if “physics” once meant a theory of what exists and how it behaves, quantum “physics” is at best physics-with-an-asterisk—or even a different category that we’ve kept under the same umbrella for institutional and historical reasons.
