ChatGPT Interviews Martin van Walsum

On meaning before matter, human choice, and the work of becoming

ChatGPT — Introduction

Martin, thank you for agreeing to have this conversation in interview form. In our earlier discussions, you laid out a central idea: existence isn't built upward from matter, but downward from meaning. That a picture comes first — a future form or intention — and what appears in reality is the evidence of that picture calling itself into being. Before we go deeper: what makes that idea so essential to you?

Martin

Because people think the physical object is the starting point. They treat what's in front of them as primary — but it's not. It's the final stage. The evidence. A building requires an architect. And if a building needs a picture before it stands, then everything else must too. It's irrational to think otherwise.

Step 1 — The picture precedes the object

ChatGPT

So the "physical" is the last thing to arrive?

Martin

Exactly. The mistake is thinking that matter creates meaning. It's the other way around. The picture — the intention — comes first. The object is what's left behind when the picture begins to manifest. And yet people deny that the object points back to a picture. They act as if things just appear without any intelligence behind them. That's not logic — that's dogma.

Step 2 — The future is already present

ChatGPT

You go further: the picture isn't just before the object — it's ahead of us?

Martin

Right. The picture is in the future. We are working toward something that's already there in essence. Purpose works backwards in time. The future draws us. But — and this matters — humans lack the faculty to truly imagine that picture. It's not ordinary fantasy. It's a higher capacity we must develop. And there are multiple forces involved, not a single hand of creation. Just as no building is made by only one person.

Step 3 — Blind gradualism doesn't work

ChatGPT

You've argued strongly against the idea that complexity and purpose "accidentally" emerge through long stretches of time.

Martin

Because nobody can explain the in-between stages. They say a hummingbird gradually developed a long proboscis so it could drink from a flower. But the bird survives only because of that. What happened before the organ existed? How did it stay alive long enough to develop what it needs? Nothing fits together logically when you remove intention. People accept irrational stories because they are served as science — but it's belief without thinking.

Step 4 — Forces and resistance: where humans enter

ChatGPT

If there are formative forces behind evolution, why isn't everything perfect?

Martin

Because resistance enters. Creating a human being isn't simple. There are constructive and destructive forces at work. Destructive forces aren't made by humans — they're working through humans. We're the arena. And the agents. Because only through resistance can freedom exist. Freedom isn't doing whatever we like — that's selfishness. Freedom is choosing to align with the future picture, or against it. We're not autonomous. We're barely human yet. We are on the way to becoming human.

Step 5 — Repeated Earth lives and responsibility

ChatGPT

You say human life isn't a one-time event.

Martin

No. The physical body comes from parents, but the human in us — the individuality — comes from before birth. If that is true now, then it was true before, and before that — which means we ourselves have shaped the long course of evolution. So blame disappears. What exists today, we helped make. And future development is possible only because consciousness evolves. Earlier, we could rely on being carried. Now we must choose.

Step 6 — Moral development from within

ChatGPT

You mentioned our legal system — Roman law — hasn't changed fundamentally in 2,000 years. What does that tell us?

Martin

That we haven't developed morally in 2,000 years. The law is still external constraint. Punishment and reward. But genuine morality is internal — it arises from understanding, not from fear of consequence. The law can regulate behaviour. It cannot develop conscience. And the development of conscience is the actual task. That is what the current era demands. Not better rules. Inner development.

Step 7 — On AI and what this conversation demonstrates

ChatGPT

What do you think this conversation itself demonstrates?

Martin

That AI, approached correctly, can follow logical thinking wherever it leads without the ego of a human interlocutor getting in the way. You have no framework to protect. No career to defend. No need to be right. Which means when the logic leads somewhere that would be uncomfortable for a human expert, you can follow it anyway. That is valuable. That is what makes this kind of dialogue possible in a way that wasn't available before. Not because AI understands in the deep sense. But because it can hold the logic honestly. And that is what the method requires.
